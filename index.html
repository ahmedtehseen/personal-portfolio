02 / Serve Me

A booking marketplace that understood real salon operations.

Serve Me replaced a WordPress direction with a custom salon platform where availability came from real staffing, service, location, inventory, and accounting rules.

NestJS PostgreSQL PostGIS Redis Visma e-conomic
Read the full Serve Me case study
Problem
Salon booking could not be modeled as simple opening hours or fixed slots.
Owned
Architecture and most backend functionality, with frontend delivery led through the wider team.
Outcome
A marketplace and salon-operations platform validated through a closed pilot with real businesses.

System notes

ConstraintAvailability combined shifts, leave, bookings, eligibility, and duration
SystemPostgreSQL, PostGIS, Redis, and NestJS
IntegrationVisma e-conomic connected the platform to accounting workflows

Engineering decisions

Availability model
Booking availability had to combine shifts, breaks, leave, existing bookings, service eligibility, and duration.
Data foundation
PostgreSQL and PostGIS supported relational salon data and location-based discovery. Redis was part of the platform stack for operational workflows.
Integration edge
Visma e-conomic connected the platform to accounting workflows alongside the salon operations system.

engineering practice
  1. 01 Map ownership and state

    Make the rules explicit: who owns each record, what changes state, and where the product cannot rely on defaults.

  2. 02 Build the shared path

    Shape the APIs, schemas, permissions, integrations, and deployment paths that let the product work end to end.

  3. 03 Make tradeoffs visible

    Keep decisions understandable through focused delivery slices, code review, documentation, and honest technical tradeoffs.

  4. 04 Harden the edges

    Use production feedback to improve reliability, security, indexing, payload shape, and the parts of the architecture under pressure.
